I cherish the slow process of reading and writing down my thoughts afterward. Processing information takes time and effort. Reading is not just a process of accepting new information or following interesting plots; rather, it is a way to think as the authors think and to feel what they feel. As with other media like games and movies, a good book can create a sense of immersion and make readers lose track of time. When I truly engage in exploring how an author's worldview and insights are embodied through word choices, metaphors, and literary devices, I can generate more profound thoughts than fast-paced reading could ever achieve.

This inspired my ideas about "slow buildings." In today's fast-paced world, buildings often function as efficient workspaces, with elevator designs aiming to move people as quickly as possible. In contrast, the circulation path in the building I designed aims to slow down visitors' pace. It helps them become aware of their surroundings and the kind of space they are moving through, incorporating a sense of mindful walking. This kind of environment can be seen as a form of technology that supports a more deliberate and slower lifestyle.

This approach suggests that in an era that easily drives people toward a purely results-driven mindset, the meaning of life is not about accumulating achievements as fast as possible or sacrificing all our time for one broad goal. Rather, the ultimate purpose of life is to see, to experience, and to explore. Therefore, not all technologies necessarily need to serve convenience and efficiency; sometimes, they should help people slow down, relieve stress, and refresh their spirits.
