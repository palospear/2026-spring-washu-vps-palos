Writing is an important practice for my psychological well-being, as it helps calm my nerves. To be honest, I get anxious easily, and mindfulness isn't always effective for me. I once heard a saying: “The only way to let things exist outside one’s body is to write them down,” and this resonates deeply with me.

During high school, I often reflected on myself because I easily got nervous when interacting with others and my environment. I would write down the insights I gained to remind myself later. For instance, I might write: “If I feel annoyed or uncertain when doing something, I need to rethink the consequences.” This gives me a positive mindset, helping me believe that failures can lead to new outcomes and that everything happens for my benefit.

Later, I tried another way to express my thoughts. One frustrating afternoon, I was too upset to write down my ideas directly. Instead, I used the pronoun “her” to record my feelings, utilizing many literary techniques I had learned from prose. For example, I wrote: “There were many hollows in her heart urgently waiting to be filled—perhaps with cotton, soda, or air, just preferably not dust. Yet, as the sun rose and set, these little fissures subtly widened once more.”

This habit is valuable to me because it allows me to review my entries later and figure out whether my issues have been resolved. I also feel peaceful when journaling all my feelings and thoughts honestly, and even creatively. My sense of fulfillment comes from being honest with myself, turning sadness into motivation for writing, and applying what I have learned from reading. It serves as a tangible record that I can review later.
