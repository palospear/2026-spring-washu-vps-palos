To be honest, the tools or theories that contributed significantly to my growth during adolescence are the MBTI and Jung's Eight Dimensions Theory. They belong to a psychological typology that divides humans into eight types based on different functional hierarchies. For instance, my MBTI type is ISTP, and my function stack is Ti-Se-Ni-Fe-Te-Si-Ne-Fi. Although some may insist on individualism and argue that people cannot be simply categorized into eight types, and that labeling oneself and others will cause bias, the Pygmalion Effect, or the Barnum Effect, typology offers me a tool and perspective to find a relatively stable self-understanding and understand others' thoughts without feeling intimidated.

I was a child who was not very confident during junior high school and could not understand why I performed poorly in studies, sometimes pursuing entertainment instead of putting in a full effort when I already felt stressed about my grades. MBTI helped me realize that it was not simply because I was not hard-working or inferior to others, but that I might lean towards Carpe diem and avoidance tendencies in the face of stress, which is a coping mechanism to adjust my mindset. Though I hadn't delved into professional knowledge at that time, this typology showed me that all people have their own advantages and disadvantages due to different function priorities.

During high school, I gradually studied Jung's Eight Dimensions Theory and the Enneagram. They helped me to understand myself and others, as well as why all those differences exist. I also utilized these tools to reflect on myself and relieve stress when interacting with my surroundings.

These psychological theories helped me build the foundation of my identity, since much of my self-exploration and self-awareness was built upon these frameworks. I really appreciate the opportunity I had to gain deeper insights into this world.
