This reading is inspiring because it is deeply relevant to website design, user experience, and navigation systems. I love the way the author describes elements in a lower visual hierarchy as something users “are not looking at directly but can perceive”, including background colors, sidebars, and subtle breathing animations. When designing my own webpage, I often focus too much on the overall interaction while easily ignoring how those subtle background elements set the entire mood and context of the site. This insight into the periphery also reminds me of the importance of making intentional trade-offs to establish a strong focal point. In my opinion, good web design is not just about cramming all possible information onto the screen; instead, peripheral elements should support rather than interrupt the user's journey. Reading about the 'dangling string' immediately made me think of loading animations. Just like the string, a good loading state quietly lets the user know what's happening without demanding their full attention. All of these revolves around the user's experience and locatedness.
